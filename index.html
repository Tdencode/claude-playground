Tokyo

Arrival: Narita Airport

Oct 1 - 6 (6 nights)

🏨 Where we stayed

APA Hotel Akasaka Mitsuke

Wouldn't really recommend it — the room was tiny, basically just a bed shoved against the wall with nowhere to sit.

✓ Location's great though — Akasaka-Mitsuke is a solid spot for food and getting around.

Things to do

  • Shibuya — trendy area with heaps of restaurants and shopping, and the famous Shibuya Crossing. While you're there, have a look at the MEGA Don Quijote (the biggest one) — it's kind of like a Japanese Costco, totally overwhelming but worth a look.
  • Akihabara — the pop culture and tech area. Heaps of anime, manga and electronics if that's your thing.
  • Shinjuku — good for nightlife, shopping and food, always something going on.
  • Tsukiji Outer Market for fresh sushi.
  • Wander through Meiji Shrine and Yoyogi Park.
Why we liked the area: the food around Akasaka-Mitsuke is really good. It's not too busy and it's super well connected — 4 metro lines — so you can head out and explore Tokyo during the day and still find great places to eat near the hotel at night.
Travel: train to Mount Mitake from Tokyo
2

Mount Mitake

Traditional mountain ryokan stay

Oct 7 (1 night)

🏨 Where we stayed

Traditional ryokan on the mountain top

⭐ Highly recommend this one — such a cool traditional experience. They did a traditional dinner and breakfast, and we had our own private onsen.

⚠️ Heads up though — the town's built into the mountain and it's seriously steep. It's a solid 20 minute walk uphill to get up there with a fair bit of climbing and lots of stairs. If you've got big or heavy bags this could genuinely be a dealbreaker, so have a think about your luggage before you commit.

Highlights

  • Staying at a traditional ryokan with a private onsen — one of the highlights of the whole trip.
  • Traditional multi-course dinner and breakfast are included.
  • Have a wander around the old temples in the mountain town.
  • Really peaceful up there, and the views are great.
⚠️ Luggage warning: the walk up to the accommodation is no joke — 20 minutes of steep uphill with heaps of stairs cut into the mountain. If you're hauling big suitcases it could be a dealbreaker. I'd honestly pack light, use a luggage forwarding service, or maybe skip this one if you can't travel light. Can't stress enough how steep it is.
Travel: train to Nagoya
3

Nagoya

Oct 8 - 9 (2 nights)

🏨 Where we stayed

Comfort Inn Nagoya Sakae

✓ Recommend it — good hotel, everything you need.

Things to do

  • Mt. Oyama day trip — train and bus to get there. There's a cable car partway up with a few restaurants and nice views. The hike to the summit is steep with a heap of stairs, but there's a little restaurant at the top as a reward.
  • Walk the Magome Pass — an old trade route through the mountains between two really old-style villages. Lovely walk, and both ends have cool little towns with good food and traditional crafts.
Food: heaps of good restaurants around the hotel.
Travel: Shinkansen to Hiroshima
4

Hiroshima

Oct 10 - 12 (2 nights)

🏨 Where we stayed

NAGI Hiroshima Hotel and Lounge

⭐ Highly recommend — one of the best places we stayed. Beautiful spot, we loved it.

Things to do

  • Shukkeien Gardens — this one's a must. Beautiful Japanese garden with koi ponds and lakes. Grab some fish food at the entrance and feed them, watching them all swarm is weirdly fun.
  • Miyajima Island — set aside a full day. Great temples, an old township to poke around, and you can hike or catch the cable car to the top for amazing views over the bay.
  • Peace Memorial Park and Museums — worth going, important bit of history.
  • Hiroshima Castle — nice to visit, we enjoyed it.
  • Okonomiyaki Tower — good for lunch, especially on your way out. It's 4 floors of okonomiyaki joints, which is the classic Hiroshima dish. Sit at the bar and watch them cook it on the hotplate right in front of you [Map]
Food: the hotel's near heaps of good restaurants. There's a camping-themed restaurant a block away on the second floor — camping chairs, camping utensils, great vibe. There's also a really good Japanese BBQ place nearby that uses all Hiroshima produce (it had just opened when we were there).
Travel: Shinkansen to Osaka
5

Osaka

Oct 12 - 18 (6 nights)

🏨 Where we stayed

Ocean Niponichi (near Dontonbori)

⚠️ Bit of a mixed bag — location is spot on but the rooms aren't great. I'd look at other options around Dontonbori.

Things to do

  • Osaka Aquarium — everyone kept telling us to go and yeah, it lived up to it. Really good.
  • Explore Dontonbori — endless little streets packed with restaurants and bars. Genuinely mad how much there is.
  • Round1 arcade — perfect if you want to embrace your inner child. Around $40 for a 24-hour pass. So good.
  • Day trip to Kyoto — walk around all the temples (heads up, it's very busy with tourists).
  • Osaka Castle — nice walk around the grounds, and you can head inside if you want. Way less crowded than Kyoto.
Why Dontonbori: this is the spot to stay in Osaka. It's a maze of tiny streets full of restaurants and bars — you could wander for days and keep finding new places.

Food You've Gotta Eat

The stuff you've gotta eat while you're there

🍣 Sushi

Recommend: Uobei

Note: sushi can get pretty pricey in Japan, so keep that in mind.

🥩 Wagyu Beef

Wagyu's everywhere. We had an amazing one in Hiroshima. A lot of markets also sell little bite-sized bits on sticks, which is a good way to try it without spending a fortune.

🥞 Okonomiyaki

There were heaps of good places near where we stayed in Akasaka-Mitsuke, but they needed reservations and we didn't sort it in time. Learn from us and book ahead.

🍜 Ramen - Ichiran ⭐

It's not the best ramen in Japan, but it's iconic and still really good — you've gotta do it at least once.

The experience: the lines are usually pretty long but it's worth it. You sit in your own little single-person booth, order off a touchscreen and a paper form, and there are wooden markers for anything else you need. No one talks and they slide your food through a curtain at the back of the booth. Really fun, and it's popular with locals, not just tourists.

Pro Tips

The Nagoya one had shorter lines than Tokyo or Osaka.

The Kyoto one is right near the main market, so a lot of people head there instead and the lines were shorter.

🐙 Takoyaki

Octopus balls. You'll spot street vendors selling these all over the place — classic street food, give it a go.

🍙 Onigiri - Convenience Stores

Where: 7/11 or any of the other convenience stores

The convenience stores in Japan are honestly great — heaps of good stuff for a quick lunch or to take with you exploring. We grabbed onigiri from here for breakfast most mornings.

🍛 Japanese Curry

We had this a fair bit — good local places everywhere. CoCo Curry House is the big chain version. It's decent, not as good as the local spots, but you order on an app and it's all quick and easy, so it's a good shout when you're tired and can't be bothered with the language barrier.

🍢 Izakaya

Basically Japanese tapas — places that do a bunch of smaller dishes like tempura, karaage (fried chicken) and loads more. They're everywhere and a great way to try a few different things in one sitting.

Things You've Gotta Do

A few things I'd make sure you do

🛍️ Don Quijote ⭐

Go into a Don Quijote at least once — the biggest is in Shibuya but any of the MEGA ones will do. It's a bit like a Japanese Costco, kind of fascinating seeing all the random stuff they sell.

Fair warning: it's overwhelming and busy — full-on sensory overload, but in a good way.

Hiroshima

Miyajima Island ⭐

Can't recommend Miyajima Island enough. It's got heaps of temples and an old township to walk around with plenty of food about. You can hike or take the cable car to the top and look out over the whole bay — the views are unreal.

Osaka ⭐

Osaka is just a cool city for like food/bars and stuff to do.

Dontonbori area (I highly recommend you stay here)

Stay in Dontonbori so you can walk around the area - it's basically just a suburb with a million tiny streets and every street is just all tiny restaurants and bars, it's crazy how endless it is and that there's so many.

Round1 Games Arcade

Round1 games arcade in Osaka is unreal if you want to embrace your inner child.

Kyoto

Kyoto is cool, but it's also super busy - like very busy. We just did a day trip from Osaka, I think you can see similar stuff elsewhere with less people. But Kyoto definitely has way more temples all near each other compared to anywhere else, so I can see why it's so popular.

Nagoya & The Magome Pass

We also stopped in Nagoya on the Shinkansen for 2 nights to walk the Magome pass. It's an old trade route through the mountains between two really old style villages, it was a very nice walk and the townships at both ends were really interesting and had good food and trinkets.

Tokyo

Where to stay: Akasaka-Mitsuke area ⭐

I'm assuming you're going to Tokyo, the real question is where to stay — it's a huge place. I did a lot of research and reckon the area between Akasaka-Mitsuke station and Akasaka station is the best spot.

It's really well connected — you've got 4 metro lines and it's pretty central. It's not super busy but still has heaps of good restaurants in walking distance, so you can jump on the trains to explore Tokyo in the day and easily grab food near your place at night.

We loved the area, it was awesome.

Areas to explore

Akihabara — pop culture & tech hub

This is the spot for pop culture and tech. If you're into anime, manga, games or electronics you've gotta go. The streets are full of multi-storey electronics shops, anime stores and arcades.

Shinjuku — entertainment & shopping

Big entertainment and shopping district with a real buzz to it. Good for nightlife, food and just wandering. You've got everything from fancy department stores to little izakayas and late-night spots.

Shibuya — trendy shopping & dining ⭐

Trendy area, great for food and shopping. It's home to the famous Shibuya Crossing — one of the busiest crossings in the world and a must-see. Loads of restaurants, cafes and shops around it, and a great place to just people-watch and take Tokyo in.

Day trips from Tokyo

Mt. Oyama

Great day trip from Tokyo by train and bus. There's a cable car partway up with a few restaurants and nice views. If you're feeling keen, hike to the summit — fair warning, it's steep with a ridiculous amount of stairs. There's a little restaurant at the top to reward yourself when you make it.

A Few Tips

Hotel size warning ⚠️

Worth knowing — a lot of the hotels are tiny (space efficient), especially in central Tokyo and Osaka. We stayed in a few APA Hotels (big chain) and it's basically just a bed against one wall with nowhere to sit. Fine for a couple of nights if you're out all day, but not great if you're staying a while and want to actually chill in the room.

So check the size of places when you book.
